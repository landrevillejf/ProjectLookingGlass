The Taskbar

The taskbar is the translucent strip along the edge of the screen. It is your window switcher and the home of the Start Menu button.

Live thumbnails

Each open window is represented by a miniature that tracks the window's actual content in real time — not a static icon. This makes it easy to recognize a window at a glance.

  • Click a thumbnail to bring that window to the front.
  • Click a minimized window's thumbnail to restore it.
  • Minimizing a window (from its title bar) collapses it back into its thumbnail.

Background

The taskbar blends into the desktop background. When you launch with the 3D model background (-b), the taskbar sits in front of the model; see Customizing the Desktop.

Tip: If you have many windows open, use the bookshelf (right-click the background) to see them all at once — see Working with Windows.

On the 2D and Swing desktops

The 2D and Swing desktops use a conventional taskbar rather than live 3D thumbnails. Alongside the open-window buttons and the Start Menu button it carries a Notifications button, live volume / network / battery indicators, and a clock that opens a calendar when you click it. See The 2D and Swing Desktops for details.
